# Eshme-Commerce project direction

## Product purpose

Help aspiring sellers get an ecommerce operation moving: research niches, discover and qualify low-commitment or consignment sourcing, organize suppliers and contacts, prepare products and selling channels, then manage campaigns, buyer conversations, orders, and follow-up.

## Product standards

- Make routine tasks convenient: safe defaults, editable starters, prefilled context, and minimal repeated typing.
- Keep claims honest. Search results and supplier terms are leads, not proof of profitability, zero-upfront cost, or consignment until dated evidence or a direct supplier response confirms them.
- Prefer free-to-use services. If a provider requires payment, preserve what works and find another option.
- The public URL is permanently `https://eshme-commerce.netlify.app/`. Deploy changes to the same Netlify project and keep old Netlify links forwarding there.
- Keep the local app, GitHub source, and live site synchronized. Verify the live page after every deployment.
- The interface should feel Apple-like and Framer-like: refined layouts, energetic scroll-linked motion, sticky storytelling, and lightweight transforms. Respect reduced-motion preferences, while letting users deliberately opt into full motion.
- Keep public language focused on the service. Do not describe it as a portfolio piece or temporary demonstration.
- The first 30 days are free; future subscription pricing is coming soon. Access remains free until billing is live, with no automatic charge.

## Communication style

The owner is direct and casual, values visible results and precise URLs, and expects honest status updates. Explain practical limitations plainly. When drafting messages in their voice, keep them short and natural; use informal terms only when they fit the recipient.

## Current implementation boundary

The app uses a public Netlify front end and sign-in, with private seller records on Netlify. Product feeds and campaign content can be prepared and exported; external stores, social publishing, inbox monitoring, and payment processing need approved provider connections before they can be described as live integrations. Public-web sourcing scans should show source links and clear verification status, not promise an exhaustive crawl.
